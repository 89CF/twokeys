02 · THE PROBLEM

A deposit to a stranger is a leap of faith

Car, real-estate and second-hand marketplaces share one weak spot: the up-front deposit.

TwoKeys Protocol · HackYeah 2026

02 / 10

1

A listing that looks too good

Car, flat or rental at a great price. The “seller” asks for a deposit to hold it.

2

Paid by bank transfer

Straight to a stranger’s IBAN. No vault, no conditions, no undo.

3

Then the seller disappears

Listing deleted, phone offline. No history to check, nobody to chase.

POLISH CIVIL CODE

“zadatek” (earnest money)

Buyer backs out → seller keeps the deposit.

Seller backs out → buyer gets double back.

The law already says who pays.

But nobody sues a stranger over a few thousand złoty.

03 · TARGET USER & DESIGN RATIONALE

Who it’s for — and what we redesigned

TwoKeys Protocol · HackYeah 2026

03 / 10

PRIMARY USER

People in Poland paying a zadatek to strangers from listing sites — and their sellers.

Not crypto users. The app says “account”, shows amounts in PLN and hides the chain.

SECONDARY USER

Marketplaces that embed the widget — our distribution channel.

1

Which relationship?

An up-front deposit paid to a stranger before the deal is completed.

2

Who was the intermediary?

Usually nobody — an IBAN transfer straight to the stranger. Or costly ones: a court to enforce art. 394, a notary escrow, a platform’s paid payment protection.

3

What changes without it?

The rule runs instantly, with no court: whoever backs out loses; a seller who walks pays 2×. Money waits in the program, not with either side. No fee, and nobody can block the payout.

04 · THE SOLUTION

THE MOMENT THE INTERMEDIARY IS NO LONGER NEEDED

Seller vanished? After the deadline, anyone can press “Apply the outcome” (claim_after_deadline). The program counts the missing party as a no-show and pays out by the rule. No court, no support ticket, no admin.
